Someone won't wake up?

  1. Check if they respond. Shout their name and rub hard on the middle of their chest with your knuckles. Look for slow, stopped or gurgling breathing, blue or gray lips, and tiny pupils.
  2. Call 911. Say the person is not breathing or won't wake up. Put the phone on speaker and follow the dispatcher.
  3. Give naloxone. Lay them on their back. Put the nozzle fully into one nostril and press the plunger firmly once. The spray does not need priming.
  4. Support breathing. If you are trained, give one rescue breath every 5 seconds. If they are not breathing normally and you cannot tell, start CPR: push hard and fast in the center of the chest, 100–120 a minute.
  5. Second dose if needed. No response after 2–3 minutes? Give another dose with a new device in the other nostril.
  6. Recovery position and stay. If they breathe on their own, roll them onto their side with the top knee bent. Stay with them: naloxone can wear off in 30–90 minutes, before the opioid does.

General guidance following SAMHSA and the FDA naloxone label. It does not replace 911.
